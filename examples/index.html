LearnKit examples

About this page

What this is. Every LearnKit building block, working, on one page. The heading above each one is its name, such as lk-quiz. Beside each one are some ideas for using it.

What you can do here

  • Try each block on the left: click it, type in it, and use the keyboard.
  • Read the ideas on the right, then follow “See it in every look” to view it in each style.
  • Note the names of the ones you like. When you ask Claude to build something, you can use those names. For example: “use an lk-quiz for the check at the end”.
  • Ignore the short pieces of code in grey. They are for Claude, and for whoever looks after the code.

lk-tabs

Each child marked with data-lk-tab becomes a tab.

Use the arrow keys, Home and End to move between tabs.

Listen for the lk-tabchange event to track progress.

Good for splitting one topic into parts the learner picks between.

Ideas for using it

  • Show the same case study from three points of view: the learner, the tutor and the employer.
  • Put "Overview", "Try it" and "Check yourself" side by side, so learners choose their own route.
  • Keep each tab short. If learners must read them in order, use a step-by-step process instead.

lk-accordion

Use the up and down arrows, Home and End to move between headings.

Yes. Add the multiple attribute.

Set level from 1 to 6. The default is 3.

Good for content learners dip into, not read from top to bottom.

Ideas for using it

  • A frequently asked questions list at the end of a unit.

  • Turn on "multiple" when learners may want to compare two answers at once.

lk-scenario

A learner who has never been late before asks for an extra week on their assignment.

  • Refuse: the deadline was clear
  • Ask why they need the time

They explain a family emergency. You can agree an extension or offer support.

  • Agree a short extension and a check-in
  • Agree the extension and say nothing more

They submit nothing and stop attending sessions.

They submit on the new date and say the check-in helped.

The work arrives, but a chance to offer support was missed.

Good for practising decisions where the choice changes what happens.

Ideas for using it

  • A difficult conversation with a learner, with several ways to respond.
  • A workplace dilemma where each choice leads somewhere different.
  • Include at least one choice that goes badly. Learners remember those.

lk-sort

  • A quiz in class
  • Feedback on a draft
  • A final exam
  • An end-of-year report

Good for deciding which group something belongs to.

Ideas for using it

  • Sort examples into "formative" and "summative", or "safe" and "unsafe".
  • Match tools, methods or people to the group they suit.
  • Drag works with a finger as well as a mouse. Everyone else uses the "Move to" list on each item.
  • Add pickup for a third way: press Pick up on an item, then Place in (group). No dragging or aiming.
